\begin{abstract}
Designing molecules that jointly satisfy multiple physicochemical properties
is a central challenge in drug discovery and materials design. Existing
conditional generators typically express molecular objectives as flat
condition vectors or instructions, leaving their behavior under increasingly
complex and out-of-distribution property combinations poorly understood. Here,
we introduce \emph{MolProgram}, a direct molecular generation framework that
represents each design request as a variable-length program of property
targets, tolerances, and directions. MolProgram combines LLM-derived
instruction features with explicit numerical program tokens and translates
their joint representation into SMILES using a compact
autoregressive decoder. A property-count curriculum emphasizes high-order
programs during supervised training. We then apply a Group Relative Policy
Optimization (GRPO)-style post-training objective that learns from groups of
sampled molecules and programmatically verifiable property feedback to improve
joint constraint satisfaction. All molecules are generated directly by the
model, without retrieval from an external molecular library, a molecular
materializer, or inference-time property reranking. By varying both program
complexity and candidate budget, we evaluate generation from low-order
objectives to difficult high-order, reversed, extreme, and rare property
combinations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that GRPO-style post-training
improves high-order and out-of-distribution control over
supervised fine-tuning, with gains already visible at modest sampling budgets.
These results establish MolProgram as a unified framework for scaling direct
molecular generation toward increasingly compositional design objectives.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Multi-property de novo molecular generation is widely used in computer-aided
drug discovery and materials design to propose candidates that satisfy several
physicochemical requirements at once. The task can be formulated as
conditional distribution learning: given a variable-length property program
$P=\{(j,y_j,\tau_j,d_j)\}_{j=1}^{m}$, learn a distribution
$p_\theta(s\mid P)$ over molecular strings $s$. Each program clause identifies
a property $j$, a target $y_j$, a tolerance $\tau_j$, and an optional direction
$d_j$; a generated string is a strict solution only if it is chemically valid
and satisfies every active clause. We study the zero-source setting, in which
no initial molecule is provided and the model must directly generate a
molecule from the requested property program. A common solution is to learn a
conditional generator from property-labeled molecules and then approximate the
design objective by drawing, guiding, or selecting candidates from
$p_\theta(s\mid P)$.

This general strategy has developed along several complementary paradigms.
Sequence generators concatenate molecular strings with property tokens or
condition vectors and decode candidates autoregressively, providing a compact
and scalable route to direct generation \citep{bagal2022molgpt}. Graph and
image generators instead
embed conditions into iterative denoising processes, allowing molecular
structure to be constructed under numerical or categorical guidance
\citep{liu2024graphdit,wang2025sketchmol}. Arbitrary-subset conditional models
use property masking, classifier-free guidance, or self-criticism to support
different subsets of available attributes
\citep{jolicoeurmartineau2024stggplus}. Instruction-tuned molecular models
replace fixed task interfaces with natural language and transfer across
multi-property objectives \citep{dey2025gellm3o,dey2025controllable}. More
recently, feedback-based methods have used molecular predictors or programmatic
property functions for selection, guidance, and reinforcement learning,
enabling the generator to optimize objectives that are difficult to capture
with supervised likelihood alone \citep{wang2025sketchmol,gao2026cmoral}.

Despite their different representations, these paradigms share several
limitations under compositional scaling. First, a variable set of objectives is
commonly collapsed into a flat condition vector or instruction, so property
count and the internal structure of a request are not explicit modeling
objects. This makes it difficult to distinguish learning individual
property--structure correlations from learning how those correlations compose
in high-order, reversed, extreme, or rarely observed combinations. Second,
natural-language instructions offer a flexible semantic interface but are an
imprecise representation of numerical targets and tolerances, whereas fixed
numerical vectors are precise but provide little structure for variable-length
composition. Third, evaluation often reports a molecule selected from many
samples without treating the candidate budget as part of the method. A
best-of-many success can therefore increase even when the probability assigned
to strict solutions remains small, obscuring whether progress comes from
conditional generation or additional search
\citep{gao2022sampleefficiency}.

Our key observation is that multi-property design is better represented as
execution of a structured, variable-length program than as response to a flat
condition. Such a program can retain the semantic context of an instruction
while exposing the identity, target, tolerance, direction, and total complexity
of every active constraint. It also makes two difficulty axes explicit:
program complexity determines what must be composed, while candidate budget
determines how much search is allowed. This view suggests that training should
both emphasize increasingly high-order programs and use verifiable feedback to
compare alternative executions of the same program.

Following this insight, we present \textbf{MolProgram}, a direct SMILES
generation framework that combines semantic instruction conditioning, explicit
numerical property programs, and group-relative policy optimization.
MolProgram first encodes the design instruction using frozen LLM-derived
features and augments them with deterministic tokens for the active property
clauses and a program summary. A compact autoregressive Transformer then
cross-attends to this joint representation and produces SMILES without
retrieval from an external molecular library or a materialization stage. During
supervised training, a property-count curriculum reweights sampling and token
loss toward high-order programs. During post-training, a Group Relative Policy
Optimization (GRPO)-style objective compares groups of candidates generated
for the same program and uses programmatically verifiable molecular-property
rewards, together with supervised and reference-policy regularization, to
update the SMILES policy \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}. The LLM condition
encoder remains frozen; GRPO-style
post-training updates the compact molecular decoder rather than the LLM itself.

We evaluate MolProgram by preserving generation order and measuring prefixes of
the same candidate stream over a range of budgets. This separates raw candidate
success from the probability that a condition is solved at least once, and
prevents property-aware selection from being presented as one-shot generation.
Across difficult six- and seven-property programs and out-of-distribution
reversed, extreme, and rare combinations, GRPO-style post-training improves
success over supervised fine-tuning at modest candidate budgets. The resulting
picture is deliberately more specific than a best-of-many headline: MolProgram
improves the probability that a small sample set contains a strict solution,
while its effect need not be uniform for the first draw.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formulate zero-source multi-property molecular generation through
  variable-length property programs and introduce MolProgram, which combines
  LLM-derived instruction conditioning, explicit numerical program tokens, a
  property-count curriculum, and GRPO-style verifier-based post-training in a
  direct SMILES generator.
  \item We develop a budget-transparent evaluation of compositional control and
  provide empirical evidence that group-relative post-training improves
  difficult high-order and out-of-distribution program satisfaction at modest
  sampling budgets, without relying on retrieval, materialization, or
  inference-time property reranking.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews
