\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Our experiments separate three questions that are often confounded in
molecular generation: whether construction and editing can share one policy,
whether the training distribution covers the requested property programs, and
whether an apparent gain comes only from a larger inference budget. We freeze
the data boundary before training, balance optimization by task rather than by
raw corpus frequency, and retain the sampling unit of each external benchmark.

\subsection{Training data and leakage-controlled capacity}
\paragraph{De novo supervision.}
We stream the first four million records from the official PubChem bulk SDF
archive and canonicalize them with RDKit. We retain single-fragment molecules
containing common drug-like elements and compute MW, LogP, QED, TPSA, HBD, HBA,
and RB. After validity, descriptor-range, duplicate, and held-out-target
filters, 3,290,815 unique molecules remain eligible. A seeded hash selects
2,000,000 targets. Each target is paired with one 2p--7p numerical property
program derived from its computed descriptors; the six arity strata contain
333,334/333,334/333,333/333,333/333,333/333,333 examples. The numerical values
are inputs during training but the target molecule is present only as the
assistant completion.

\paragraph{Editing supervision.}
The isolated MolEdit-Instruct training split contains 2,984,459 rows, but row
count is not edit-pair capacity: 1,548,544 rows repeat a canonical
source--target pair under another instruction view. We additionally remove
767,651 rows whose canonical source or target molecule occurs in the frozen
50k editing split or a paper reference. This molecule-level boundary prevents
memorizing a held-out target through another source. It leaves 668,262 valid,
non-identity unique pairs with at least one verified descriptor change. We form
directional programs only from source--target descriptor differences that pass
the train-only activity rule; unverified assay text is retained as provenance
but is not promoted to a label.

We next allocate unique pairs to edit arities 1p--7p. The 7p stratum is the
limiting stratum, with 81,417 assignable pairs after isolation and
deduplication. Consequently, the leakage-controlled release contains exactly
81,417 pairs per edit arity, or 569,919 edit examples. We do not inflate this
number to two million by repeating pairs: doing so would make frequent
instruction views, rather than independent molecular transformations, define
the effective objective. The resulting release, \emph{MolProgramInstruct-
Balanced}, contains 2,569,919 examples and separately reports instruction,
unique-molecule, and unique-pair counts.

\paragraph{Why task balance, not row balance.}
The release is intentionally larger on the de novo side because PubChem
provides more independent structures. Optimization does not inherit this
imbalance. We define thirteen broad task buckets---six de novo arities (2p--7p)
and seven editing arities (1p--7p)---and use deterministic round-robin sampling
without replacement within a bucket. The common non-replacement capacity is
\begin{equation}
 n_{\rm bal}=13\min_b |\mathcal D_b|
 =13\times81{,}417=1{,}058{,}421.
 \label{eq:balanced-training-capacity}
\end{equation}
Every prefix differs by at most one example across tasks. This gives editing
seven thirteenths of the updates even though it contributes fewer released
rows, and prevents easy low-order programs from dominating high-order control.

\subsection{Training schedule and checkpoint policy}
MolProgram uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and one trainable LoRA adapter for both
modes. We initialize the scale continuation from the frozen aligned-24k
checkpoint, which already establishes the optional-source interface and the
multi-negative preference objective. The large-corpus stage is positive SFT
with BF16, maximum sequence length 448, microbatch size one, learning rate
$10^{-5}$, seed 24003, and checkpoints every 500 optimizer steps. Its
operational gate uses 500 steps with gradient accumulation 26, corresponding
to exactly 1,000 examples from each of the thirteen tasks. The gate checks
finite adapter weights, parse validity, non-identity edits, and source-copy
rate; benchmark accuracy is not used for model selection.

After the gate passes, the full broad stage uses 16,283 optimizer steps and
gradient accumulation 65. Because 65 is five complete thirteen-task cycles,
the run observes exactly 81,415 examples per task (1,058,395 total), leaving
only two examples per bucket unused for batch divisibility. Checkpoint resume
preserves the sampler seed and byte-offset indices, so a Slurm timeout changes
neither data order nor task counts.

\paragraph{Low-rate task-alignment refresh.}
The large corpus supplies broad physicochemical control but cannot manufacture
reliable GSK3B, DRD2, or SA labels from descriptor deltas. Replacing these
scarce objectives with unrelated properties would contradict the claimed task
alignment. We therefore finish with an explicitly separated refresh drawn from
the frozen aligned-24k training split. It contains 720 examples for each of the
six de novo arities and each of the ten Table~2 edit families (11,520 rows
total), selected by frozen hash rank and interleaved with equal quotas. Starting
from the broad-stage adapter, the refresh runs for one epoch at
$5\times10^{-6}$. Broad scale therefore teaches the common molecular language,
while the low-rate refresh restores rare benchmark semantics without claiming
that they exist at PubChem scale.

We freeze the final adapter hash before paper evaluation. The aligned-24k
checkpoint remains a controlled small-data diagnostic; the balanced-scale
checkpoint uses identical prompts, generation code, and scorers so that a
change cannot be attributed to a revised test contract.

\subsection{Frozen evaluation sets}
The multi-property de novo benchmark contains 6,000 SketchMol conditions, with
1,000 conditions for each arity from 2p through 7p
\citep{wang2025sketchmol}. The main 2p--4p comparison and separate 6p/7p stress
test are selected by seeded hash rank from this frozen pool. Editing is
evaluated on the ten MolEdit-Instruct task families
\citep{zhuang2026moleditrl}; no training source or target molecule occurs in
the evaluation references. The OOD set contains the twelve numerical targets
from SketchMol Supplementary Table~3: LogP $\{8,9,10\}$, TPSA
$\{160,170,180\}$, HBA $\{11,12,13\}$, and RB $\{11,12,13\}$. These targets
are outside the training range and are never used for checkpoint selection.
All reference files, prompts, adapter weights, and generation manifests are
hash-locked before scoring.

\subsection{Generation budgets and fair comparison}
At inference, MolProgram receives only the property program and optional source
molecule. It never observes a target molecule, target property values, a
property oracle, or a static candidate pool. De novo evaluation draws 40
candidates per condition and applies the declared SketchMol-compatible
property finalizer; 40 matches the official SketchMol conditional sampling
budget. Editing emits eight candidates in raw order and applies no
property-aware reranking. The external comparison treats all eight editing
outputs as candidate-level observations. Raw@1 and Any@$K$ are reported only
as within-model diagnostics and are never substituted for candidate-level
accuracy. OOD evaluation likewise scores 40 raw candidates per target without
property-aware selection.

\subsection{Metrics and reporting conventions}
For de novo design, strict success requires every active property clause to
pass under the frozen benchmark tolerances. For editing, we report validity and
$\operatorname{Acc}^{\rm cand@8}_{\rm all}(t)$ and
$\operatorname{Acc}^{\rm cand@8}_{\rm valid}(t)$ at source-Tanimoto thresholds
$t\in\{.65,.15\}$. The former counts invalid outputs as failures; the latter
conditions on validity. $\operatorname{AnyAcc}^{@K}_{\rm all}(t)$ is a
condition-level prefix statistic and is not compared with the output-level
MolEditRL endpoint.

For OOD extrapolation, Validity is the fraction of raw candidates that decode
to molecules, Success-valid conditions property success on valid molecules,
and Strict-all counts invalid generations as failures. The SketchMol Strict-all
macro average is reconstructed from the twelve per-target products of validity
and Success-valid, rather than from the product of their macro averages. All
external rows retain their published metrics and sample counts. A dash denotes
an unavailable matched experiment, not a zero.
