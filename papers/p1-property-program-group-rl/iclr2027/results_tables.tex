\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Evaluation contract}
We freeze the P17 strong anti-copy continuation and the P18 validity-aware
multi-negative continuation before evaluation. P21 starts from the exact P18
adapter and applies the group-relative update in
Equation~\eqref{eq:group-policy-objective}; its training set and protocol are
locked before any P21 evaluation. All three checkpoints use the same
Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, prompt, tokenizer, response schema, and generation seeds.
The MolEdit-Instruct pilot contains 100 conditions, ten from each of ten tasks.
The de novo pilot contains 100 locked conditions for each of 2p, 3p, and 4p;
the hard audit contains 20 six-property and 20 seven-property conditions.
Training-source and training-target overlap with the evaluation sets is zero.

All editing budgets are ordered raw prefixes: one greedy candidate followed by
seven sampled candidates. No target access or property reranking is used.
De novo generation likewise has no target access, but its matched headline
metric applies the SketchMol property-aware finalizer after 40 candidates.
Because these endpoints answer different questions, we display them side by
side but never average them.

\subsection{The shared checkpoint improves budgeted editing}
Table~\ref{tab:editing-budget} expands the editing half of
Table~\ref{tab:main-unified}. At $K=1$, the strict difference is only $+0.01$
and its paired-bootstrap interval crosses zero. At $K=4$ and $K=8$, P18
improves strict success by $+0.10$ and $+0.15$, with task-stratified paired
bootstrap 95\% intervals $[0.02,0.18]$ and $[0.07,0.24]$. Best source
similarity also rises at both budgets. Property-only Any@$K$ is not higher,
which indicates that the strict gain comes from a better property--fidelity
balance rather than from property success alone.

The task-level breakdown in Table~\ref{tab:editing-by-task} shows that the
macro improvement is not uniform. P18 is especially helpful for MW-up and the
HBA-down/SA-down task, but is weaker on the three-property DRD2 task at small
budgets. On the expanded ten-condition stratum, this deficit disappears at
$K=8$ (both checkpoints reach 0.40), while GSK3B-up remains unsolved. We
therefore claim improved budgeted editing on the frozen macro, not dominance
on every task or at greedy decoding.

\subsection{De novo transfer is mixed under the fair 40-candidate budget}
Table~\ref{tab:denovo-2p4p} reports the corrected matched-budget de novo
result. P18 reaches 0.79, 0.65, and 0.46 on 2p, 3p, and 4p. Relative to P17,
it loses three points on 2p and six points on 3p, but gains three points on 4p.
The overall 2p--4p mean falls from 0.653 to 0.633. Thus, the editing gain is
accompanied by measurable but limited de novo interference.

\subsection{High-order construction remains unresolved}
On the expanded 40-condition hard audit, both P17 and P18 solve exactly one of
20 six-property conditions by $K=8$ and neither solves a seven-property
condition. P18 has higher K=8 validity (0.894 versus 0.856 overall), but lower
mean-best property satisfaction (0.586 versus 0.616). The full audit appears
in Table~\ref{tab:hard-denovo}. It does not support a hard de novo improvement
claim and motivates balancing or group-relative post-training rather than
post-hoc selection of a favorable stratum.

\subsection{Locked test of group-relative post-training}
Table~\ref{tab:group-relative-gate} isolates the question introduced by the
P18 diagnostics: can training on relative outcomes increase strict raw-prefix
success without losing validity? The primary editing endpoint is strict
$\operatorname{AnyAcc}_{\rm all}^{@8}(.65)$ on the same 100 locked conditions.
The high-order endpoint uses the same 40 locked 6p/7p programs and no
property-aware reranking. P21 is promoted only if it improves strict editing at
$K=1$ or $K=8$ without violating the preregistered greedy-validity gates; hard
de novo behavior is reported regardless of direction. Values remain blank
until the frozen job chain completes.

Taken together, the frozen evidence supports a narrow conclusion: one
checkpoint can improve source-conditioned editing while retaining useful de
novo behavior, but sharing creates nonuniform interference. The locked P21
comparison tests whether verifier-aligned relative feedback reduces this gap.
Until that row is populated, the completed evidence supports model-level
unification, not a claim that group-relative post-training improves both
regimes or that the complete pipeline is end-to-end unified.
