\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the main results around three questions. First, how does
MolProgram compare with prior methods under established multi-property
benchmarks? Second, does group-relative post-training improve direct
generation at practical candidate budgets rather than only under large
best-of-$k$ search? Third, does the gain persist as the number of simultaneous
constraints increases? Unless stated otherwise, MolProgram uses one frozen
evaluation seed, preserves generation order, and never reports a
property-reranked candidate as one-shot generation.

We then evaluate zero-source generation under the SketchMol multi-property
protocol. For each of 1,000 held-out property vectors at each complexity level,
a method receives 40 generation attempts and succeeds if at least one valid
molecule satisfies every active property within the benchmark tolerance.
Table~\ref{tab:sketchmol-fair40} therefore compares the two methods under the
same target construction, property tolerance, and candidate budget. MolProgram
is substantially stronger on two- and three-property programs and remains
within 0.9 points at four properties. SketchMol is stronger from five
properties onward and leads
the overall average by 5.0 points. The comparison identifies high-order
composition, rather than low-order conditional control, as the principal
remaining gap.

\subsection{Group-relative post-training improves practical-budget generation}
\label{sec:results-budget}

Table~\ref{tab:budget-scaling} compares SFT and Group-RL on 6,000
two-to-seven-property programs and 1,000 OOD programs. The Group-RL gain is
already visible with small candidate groups. On 2p--7p, first-draw strict
success increases from 7.02\% to 8.93\%, while Pass@8 and Pass@20 improve by
6.35 and 7.64 points, respectively. OOD is more nuanced: SFT is slightly
better on the first draw, but Group-RL overtakes it by $k=8$ and improves
Pass@20 from 35.20\% to 41.70\%. The benefit is therefore not a
best-of-256-only effect, although it is not uniform at $k=1$.

\subsection{The gain persists with increasing program complexity}
\label{sec:results-complexity}

Absolute success decreases monotonically with program length for both models,
confirming that property count exposes a genuine compositional difficulty
axis. Nevertheless, Table~\ref{tab:complexity-scaling} shows that Group-RL
improves every property count at both practical budgets. At $k=20$, it adds
4.9 points on 6p and 8.4 points on 7p programs; the largest improvement is
11.8 points on 5p. Thus the aggregate gain cannot be explained by easy
two-property conditions dominating the benchmark.

Together, these results show a consistent but bounded advantage: Group-RL
raises small-budget success across the full compositional axis, while
high-order de novo generation, raw SMILES validity, and source preservation
remain open limitations. Full validity diagnostics, negative ablations,
MolEdit-Instruct comparisons, and paired decoder/editing repairs are reported
in the appendix rather than mixed into the main result narrative. In
particular, syntax-safe decoding substantially improves raw validity and
low-budget OOD success, but does not meet its preregistered promotion gate or
repair the small high-order OOD subset.
