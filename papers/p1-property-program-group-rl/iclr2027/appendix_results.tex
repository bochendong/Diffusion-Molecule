\subsection{Larger rollout groups are not sufficient}
\label{sec:appendix-g64}

Table~\ref{tab:g64-ablation} compares the main group-16 policy with a
hard-only continuation using 64 rollouts per program on a fixed 6p/7p subset.
The larger group improves validity but reduces Pass@20 from 50.78\% to 45.31\%,
driven by a large 7p regression. We therefore retain group 16 and report group
64 as a negative ablation rather than selecting it on raw validity.

\subsection{MolEdit-Instruct comparison}
\label{sec:appendix-moledit}

The complete ten-task comparison is reported in
Table~\ref{tab:moledit-full} rather than compressed into a macro-average.
This exposes task-level failures under the matched 20-candidate, candidate-wise
protocol. Raw@1 and pass@$k$ remain separate budget diagnostics. The completed
P4 distillation pilot also reaches zero strict
$\operatorname{Acc}_{\mathrm{all}}(.65)$ across its 20 inputs per task. Its
candidate-level mean validity is 32.5\% and its relaxed-threshold
$\operatorname{Acc}_{\mathrm{all}}(.15)$ is 7.5\%, below the frozen base
checkpoint's 36.8\% and 11.8\%, respectively. Teacher coverage reaches 77\%,
and the checkpoint audit confirms that both SFT and Group-RL leave the de novo
path bit-identical; the failure is therefore localized to the attempted
source-conditioned editing transfer. Because the pilot uses a smaller
evaluation set than the published comparison, these values do not populate
Table~\ref{tab:moledit-full}.

\subsection{MuMO source-fidelity diagnostic}
\label{sec:appendix-mumo}

Under the official 20-candidate MuMO oracle, MolProgram reaches 50.4\% overall
property SR, with 32.3\% IND and 68.8\% OOD SR. However, mean source
similarity among successful outputs is only 0.098, and no input passes the
additional 0.4 source-similarity diagnostic. The policy therefore often finds
a molecule with the desired properties by changing the molecular identity
rather than editing the input locally. MuMO SR is useful evidence for property
control, but it must not be presented as source-preserving editing success.

\subsection{Executable actions improve editing reachability}
\label{sec:appendix-actions}

A source-similarity reward applied to free-form direct SMILES Group-RL did not
produce any candidates above the required source-similarity threshold.
Replacing free-form editing with executable GraphEditDSL actions makes every
raw first candidate valid and gives 26.5\%
$\operatorname{Acc}_{\mathrm{all}}(.65)$ on the paired repair evaluation.
This large gain in reachability motivates action-based editing, but it is a
separate validation result rather than part of the main MolProgram claim.

\subsection{Syntax-safe decoding and source-consistency repair}
\label{sec:appendix-repair}

We next evaluate two frozen, single-seed repairs. Syntax-safe decoding changes
the autoregressive decoder itself rather than filtering invalid outputs after
generation. Source-consistency ranking uses only the input molecule,
scaffold retention, and local edit magnitude; it never observes a target
molecule or output-property oracle.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Paired syntax-safe decoding evaluation (\%). Each row uses the
  same frozen condition subset for the legacy and repaired decoders.}
  \label{tab:validity-repair}
  \small
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begin{tabular}{llrrrrr}
    \toprule
    Evaluation & Decoder & Valid@1 & Strict@1 & Pass@8 & Valid@20 & Pass@20 \\
    \midrule
    2p--7p & Legacy & 34.9 & 10.2 & 43.8 & 34.5 & 60.2 \\
    2p--7p & Syntax-safe & \textbf{48.2} & 10.2 & \textbf{51.6} & \textbf{50.5} & \textbf{68.5} \\
    OOD & Legacy & 12.0 & 3.3 & 26.7 & 13.1 & 46.3 \\
    OOD & Syntax-safe & \textbf{29.0} & \textbf{9.7} & \textbf{42.7} & \textbf{30.1} & \textbf{61.7} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Syntax-safe decoding raises first-draw validity by 13.3 points on 2p--7p and
17.0 points on OOD, while improving Pass@8 by 7.8 and 16.0 points,
respectively. It nevertheless misses the preregistered $+20$-point validity
promotion gate. Moreover, the aggregate OOD gain is driven mainly by the
forward- and reverse-direction subsets: on the small OOD 7p subset,
Pass@20 falls from 18.2\% to 4.5\%. We therefore report the repair as a
positive decoder ablation, not as evidence that high-order extrapolation has
been solved.

an assisted-selection result. The preregistered raw-edit gate therefore fails,
and we do not promote this variant as a new editing policy.
